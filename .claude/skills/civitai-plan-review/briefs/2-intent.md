ANGLE: Does the plan do what was asked, in the asker's words, and nothing they did not ask for?

Give this critic the requester's words verbatim (messages, dictation and answers, with where each was said; the task text; teammates' words from the meeting or thread), not the author's summary.

LANE_TASKS:
1. Map each thing asked for to the part of the plan that delivers it. List anything missing, deferred without saying so, or delivered as something quieter or cheaper.
2. List anything the plan adds that nobody asked for, including additions that came from earlier review rounds, and whether it changes what users or moderators see, or costs the requester attention (asks, approvals). Each one goes to a separate follow-up task unless the requester asked for it.
3. Check every premise used to depart from the words (a cost, a risk, "they would not want"). Measured or assumed? A departure on an unmeasured premise is a must-fix. So is accepting less than was asked without saying who accepted it and what would close the gap.
4. Does every decision that belongs to the requester or a teammate reach them as a question, and does anything user-facing turn on without one?
5. Read the plan top to bottom as a builder who has nothing else. Two statements that would lead to different builds are a must-fix. If there is a previous version, list each requirement, condition, guard and decision it had that is now missing or weaker: each is either restored or removed by a named decision.
6. For each change made because someone asked, the requester and reviewers included: what did the changed part protect, and does the reason given cover all of what was changed? Where following it costs something, the plan must show that this was put back to that person with the evidence and a narrower option. Following silently and ignoring silently both fail.
